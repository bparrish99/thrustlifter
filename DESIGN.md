# THRUSTLIFTER — Design

A tribute to the *feel* of the BBC Micro's Thrust, flown in a helicopter. Fly down,
pick up a heavy orb on a rigid rod, and lug it through increasingly tight terrain
to an orb bowl. The orb swings like a pendulum under the same gravity you fight,
and its momentum pushes and pulls the craft.

**Core principle: flying with the orb is the hardest part of the game. Combat never is,
no matter how hard a level gets.** Every system gets judged against that.

## Decided

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Platform | Browser, TypeScript, Vite. Canvas2D faux-vector glow for now (renderer isolated so a WebGL bloom pass can replace it). |
| Tilt | Limited to **±50°** (started at ±30°, opened up in playtesting). Input moves a *target* angle; actual tilt **eases** toward it. **No auto-level** (precise held angles matter in tight spaces). May revisit after playtesting. |
| Rotor | Thrust along the rotor normal. **Instant**: press = thrust, release = none (spool lag was tried and rejected because it made delicate moves too hard). Gamepad trigger gives analog throttle. Blades still *look* like they spin up. |
| Facing | Separate from tilt; flip at any time. Realistic nose geometry: tilt right + face right = nose down, missile fires slightly down. Flip to face left = nose up, fires up-left. |
| Dive | ↓ / S adds straight-down acceleration (world space, not along the rotor). Deliberately arcade, not realistic. Tunable. Shown by streaks blasting up out of the rotor (the downward version for normal thrust was rejected). |
| Missile (fire) | Fires straight out of the nose. Aim with tilt plus facing. |
| Bombs | **Removed for now** (one fire button is enough; Z fires, X flips). Possible later as upgrades, e.g. triple shot or firing perpendicular. |
| Tether | **Rigid rod** (rope makes it too easy). The rod is a true two-way constraint: the orb drags and shoves the heli. |
| Pickup | Hold tether (Space) within rod length of the orb, then pull away. After a release you must let go of Space before you can tether again. The rod locks at full length (Thrust-style; no "tractor beam" as a separate system). |
| Delivery | A small **orb bowl** (cup radius 28 vs orb radius 16). **Space while carrying releases the orb**, which keeps its momentum, so drops and "layups" work. **The bowl is the only thing the orb can touch safely**: it bounces around in the cup and is delivered once it **comes to rest in the bottom**. **The orb can't break on the bowl at all** ("magical material"; owner's call once big bowls made long lobs a thing: a hard throw into a big bowl used to shatter it). It used to shatter above a **max impact** (240), which is gone from the tuning panel. Rock, turrets, tanks and shots still destroy it. Because it can now arrive fast and bounce high, the rim tops slope down into the cup, and an orb that comes to rest on the rim gets tipped in. Lowering it onto the bowl on the rod also works (a gentle touch auto-releases). |
| Orb destruction | The orb **explodes if it touches anything except the bowl** (ground, turrets), carried or dropped, **or if it's shot** (by anything, including your own weapons). **The pilot does not die.** The orb respawns on its pedestal after ~2s. |
| Mission complete | Choplifter-style: after the orb is in the bowl, **fly back and land on your start pad**. A HUD hint points the way home. |
| Collision | Heli touching anything = death (terrain, turrets, a loose orb). The exception is skids landing gently and level on a pad. The rod passes through terrain for now. The rod crossing the rotor = "rotor strike" death. |
| Shield | **Space does double duty:** hold = shield + tether beam + fuel-tank suction (Thrust-style) when not carrying; while carrying, no shield, tap = release. The shield absorbs enemy shots (not terrain). **Each Space press does exactly one thing, decided the moment it goes down, and never switches until released:** orb in reach → tether; else fuel tank in reach → refuel; else → shield; carrying → release. (E.g. a shield press stays a shield even over a tank; a tether press does nothing once the orb drifts out of reach.) |
| Fuel | Thrust (incl. dive) and the shield burn fuel; out of fuel = no thrust/shield. **Refuel Thrust-style from fuel tanks**: hover over one and hold Space to suck it up (limited amount; empties and vanishes; can be shot). **No refuel on death**, except topping up to the respawn point's minimum (see No soft-locks). **Each survivor who climbs aboard adds a little fuel**; pads never refuel. Low-fuel warning beeps. Tunable ("Fuel & shield" folder). |
| Survivors | Choplifter-style **optional bonus**: little figures waiting near green **rescue pads** (pads exist only for rescues; each survivor picked up brings a small fuel boost) wave at you, run aboard when you land nearby, and **ride with you until mission complete** (landing on your pad mid-level doesn't unload them); they climb out one by one in the end-of-level tally, each paying 500. Crash with them aboard and they're lost. The HUD shows how many you're carrying (figure + x/y); the tally shows each one paid. |
| Safe start | **Until you lift off from the launch pad** (at level start and after every pad respawn), enemies hold fire, guards stay parked, and any shot already in flight fizzles against you. |
| No soft-locks | Every respawn point has a **minimum fuel** in level data (`startFuelMin` for the launch pad, `checkpoints[].fuel` for each checkpoint), sized so the next stretch is always possible. **Landing with an empty tank strands you**: after ~2.5s it ends the attempt (counts as a death, no explosion, "STRANDED"), anyone aboard climbs back out, and you respawn at the nearest respawn point with its minimum fuel. New levels must set both minimums (tested). |
| Checkpoints | **Internal, never announced.** A level can have **any number of checkpoints** (owner's call, 2026-11-26; there used to be one, used only after delivery). **When you die you respawn at whichever respawn point is closest to where you died**: the launch pad or any checkpoint, measured by **flying distance** through open air (so a checkpoint just the other side of a rock wall doesn't count as close). On the pad you sit landed; at a checkpoint you **hover, held in place until you touch a control** (turrets hold fire meanwhile). **Die carrying the orb and you respawn holding it** (owner's call, 2026-11-26): at the nearest checkpoint (never the launch pad, since the orb can't hang under a landed heli; checkpoints without room below for the rod are skipped), the orb hanging below you, both frozen until you touch a control; gates stay as they were (a gate that sealed on pickup stays sealed), and "nearest" treats closed gates as walls so you can't come back on the wrong side of one. Dying *without* the orb before delivery still puts it back on its pedestal and resets gates. A level with no checkpoints gets one above the bowl. The old "crash, hop, land" exploit (dying after delivery to skip the trip home) stays closed as long as checkpoints cover the way back. |
| Out of scope | No reactor, no inverse gravity. |
| Gravity | Gentle, Thrust-like. Playtest-tuned defaults: gravity 230, thrust 645, max horizontal speed 335, dive 170, max tilt 50°. The first defaults (320/850) were far too violent. With the orb, full thrust holds altitude up to ~43° of tilt; past that (up to the 50° max) you sink. |
| Turrets | Mount on **any surface** (floor, slope, wall, ceiling, pad top) and align to it automatically. Armoured gun emplacements (bolted base plate, faceted metal dome, twin barrels with a muzzle block that glow when charging, a sensor slit that blinks fast while tracking). Big enough to hit with dumb-fire missiles (generous hit area). They track you, **telegraph each shot with a visible charge-up glow**, then fire a slow, dodgeable shot. They need line of sight, range and a firing arc around the way they face, so they can sit on floors, ceilings or walls. A hit kills you or destroys the orb. Range, interval, charge time, shot speed, aim lead and arc are tunable ("Enemies" folder). Combat stays secondary to flying. **Placement rule: only put turrets where the player can dodge and shoot back.** You can't fire upward (missiles stay within ±45° of horizontal), and there are no shields yet, so no turrets above you in vertical shafts or in spots you're forced through. |
| Guard helicopters | Red enemy helis **parked on rescue pads, holding the survivors hostage**. Each rescue pad has a **set number of guards** (`guards` in level data; 0 = unguarded, mainly for the tutorial level; the editor shows a "Guards" number, 1 for a new rescue pad). (Briefly tried: only "none or endless"; the owner went back to counts.) when you come within range with line of sight, one spins up, lifts off and hunts you. **Smart and aggressive:** a nav grid of open air lets it route around rock and through tunnels; it's pushed off walls and slides along them rather than crashing; it circles you at standoff range preferring spots above you with a clear shot (dropping level or below in low caves); out of sight it keeps tracking you through the caves by sound for a few seconds (`enemyChase`); it keeps personal space so it doesn't ram you; it telegraphs then fires short bursts (`enemyBurst`) with aim lead (`enemyLead`); leashed to its pad. One missile downs one; the next appears after a delay (but never while you're within the spawn clearance of its pad, so it can't pop up right next to you), until they're all gone and the pad is clear. **Once a pad's survivors have been picked up, no more guards come** (one already flying keeps fighting). They die hitting terrain; colliding with one kills you (the orb destroys them too, and is lost). `guardsAfter: delivery` keeps a pad's guards asleep until the orb is delivered (keeps the carry enemy-free). Tunable in "Enemies". |
| Balloons | Angular, armoured enemy gasbags (faceted red body, fins, top spike, a glowing red eye slit that glances at you and flares when charging, rigid strut to a hex gun pod; classic hot-air look rejected) drift in a slow loop around their spot (`patrol`), bobbing, with a swivelling gun under the basket that tracks you, telegraphs with a glow, and fires when it sees you in range. One missile pops one. Touching one kills you; the orb pops one but is lost. Tunable in "Enemies". Level 2 keeps them on the surface so the carry stays enemy-free (tested). |
| Gates | Energy barriers in level data: `closes: pickup` seals the way in when you grab the orb; `opens: delivery` opens a way out when it's delivered; `closes: delivery` (added 2026-11-26) starts open and seals when the orb is delivered. **A sealing gate never closes on you**: if the heli or the orb is in it, it waits until you're clear, then seals. They reset if you die before delivering. This is how levels force a separate return path. |
| Levels | Played in order (`src/levels/index.ts`). N goes to the next level after completing one; 1-9 jump to any level. Level 1 Proving Ground (surface); Level 2 Deep Caverns: the orb sits right at the bottom of the first shaft, then a long winding carry (passage, drop shaft, lower tunnel, climb, big chamber) to the bowl with **no enemies on the carry**; turrets guard only the way in and the way home (sealing gate on pickup, exit gate up a separate shaft on delivery). **Level-design rule: the orb comes early, and the orb-to-bowl stretch is the main challenge.** Cave levels set `backdropY` so open cave reads dark against solid rock. |
| Sound | Procedural (Web Audio, no samples). Rotor chop tempo follows the rotor: a brisk, soft but clearly audible idle chop (~8/s) building to a harder chop (~15/s) at full throttle, over a steady drone with a light low rumble and faint turbine whine. Treble kept soft (it read as a machine gun when bright). The idle chop must sit in an audible band, not just sub-bass. M mutes; volume in the tuning panel. Event effects are synthesized too, and must have **weight, depth and sustain** (thin, short "bubble-wrap pops" were rejected). They're layered: explosions = crack + darkening fiery body + long brown-noise rumble + sub-bass drop; metal rings for clunks and debris; a rising "sucking" hum (boooeeee...) swoops up while the tether beam pulls, ending in an "...up!" chirp at lock-on (phaser tones were tried and rejected). All effects go through a hall reverb and a compressor and ring out for 1–3.5s. Distant events play quieter. |
| HUD | **Compact, in slightly translucent boxes, no labels** (owner's call, 2026-11-26; replaced the earlier big, flashy layout in neon corner brackets with a bottom-centre fuel bar). Both top boxes are **a single line**. Top-left: **score** (pops when it changes) then **lives** (small heli icons; 1UP! flash). Top-right: the **TIME BONUS countdown**, **a little hostage figure + x/y = hostages you're carrying right now / the level's total** (owner's call; it briefly counted rescues delivered, and a row of figures for those aboard was tried and dropped), then the **ORB lamp** (an empty ring; glows and pulses once delivered). Bottom-left, in its own translucent box: a **big vertical LED fuel gauge** (empty at the bottom, full at the top; F/E marks to its right; it briefly sat inside the top-right box, then the owner moved it here, enlarged it, and took it down 25%; about 255px tall on a big screen): realistic LED lenses (hot core, specular glint, halo; unlit lenses still catch light), red to amber to yellow to green, blinks when low, a light sweeps up it while refuelling. The words SCORE, TIME BONUS, RESCUED and ORB were removed from the boxes (the end-of-level tally still names its lines). A **minimap** bottom-right, in neon corner brackets, **no text**: the whole level's rock in miniature (the far-off border rock is cropped away), a blinking yellow dot for you, a magenta dot for the orb (it travels with you when carried, disappears once delivered), the bowl, your pad (green), rescue pads (yellow), fuel tanks, closed gates, and a faint box showing what's on screen. **Enemies are not shown** (tried, rejected: gives too much away). Big centre messages for events; each level opens with its name. (A round analog dial was tried and rejected.) |
| Progression | After the mission-complete tally and a ~3s pause on the results, the **next level starts automatically** (lives and score carry over). After the last level it stays on the results (R to play again). |
| Lives | Start with **3**; each death (crash or stranded) costs one; at zero → **GAME OVER** (R retries the level with 3 lives). Lives and score carry to the next level. Extra life every 10,000 points. |
| Score | Turret **100**, balloon **200**, guard heli **300** (player kills: missiles or the orb), **500** per survivor rescued (**hostages stay aboard until mission complete**: landing on your pad mid-level doesn't unload them; they climb out one by one in the end-of-level tally, which pays them; so they only score if you finish, and a crash at any point after pickup loses them), and **10 per tenth of a second of TIME BONUS left** at mission complete. Bonus time starts at the level's `targetTime` (L1 90s, L2 150s for now), **counts down only once you first lift off**, and just stops at zero (no penalty). The HUD timer shows only this countdown, labelled TIME BONUS. Plus **25 per whole tick of fuel left** on the 24-LED gauge. **No on-screen "+N" popups**; instead, on mission complete a **bonus tally** plays in the centre of the screen: first a **RESCUE BONUS** line pays each rescued hostage one at a time, slowly (~0.7s each: its figure lights up and pops, the score jumps 500, a warm rising chord), then the TIME BONUS clock drains into the score (rising blips), then the fuel gauge drains tick by tick (a climbing "ka-ching" per tick), then a final chord and summary. **Extra life every 10,000 points** (1UP! flash + arpeggio). |
| Sky | Dark gradient with twinkling stars and a faint, generated **nebula** (wispy magenta/violet/teal/amber clouds with dark dust lanes, slow parallax) behind them. The cartoon ringed planet was removed. Cave levels' dark backdrop covers it underground. |
| Ground | Solid rock has **body**: a generated tileable stone texture (mottled, veined, relief-shaded, grain) darkening with depth, with the neon surface edge and a lit band just inside it. (Neon line decorations inside the rock were tried and rejected.) |
| Pads | Your launch/return pad is **steady green**. Rescue pads are enemy pads: **blinking red**, turning **blinking yellow** once you're within the guard spawn clearance (no new guard will appear). |
| Orb pedestal | The orb starts on a glowing magenta-accented plinth generated under its start position (levels only place the orb). Solid like terrain; the glow dims once the orb is lifted. |
| Labels | **Show, don't tell.** The only centre messages are the level name at the start, MISSION COMPLETE (with the bonus tally) and GAME OVER; everything else (crashes, orb secured/destroyed/delivered, rescues, stranded) is shown through visuals and sound. **No text on level elements** (no ORB BOWL / RESCUE / FUEL words); shapes and colours carry the meaning. Text lives only in the HUD and centre messages. **Exception (owner's call, 2026-11-26): the first level in the play order has tutorial hints** (see below). |
| Look | Colourful faux-vector 80s arcade with modern glitz: additive glow lines, bloom, particles, scanlines, vignette. |

## Core loop

Launch from your pad → fight through the level → grab the orb → deliver it to the bowl → make your way back and land on your pad.

Level-design hook: the return trip doesn't have to retrace the outbound route. Levels can force a **separate path from the bowl back to the pad** (one-way drops, gates that open on delivery, a route that only works without the orb's extra length and weight).

## First-level tutorial

Level 1 only (whatever level is first in the play order; also when test-flying it from the editor). Short two-line hints in a small yellow-framed box float above the thing they're about, with an arrow pointing down at it, the first time you meet it. **Each hint is independent** (owner's call): it shows whenever its own situation applies, whatever other hints are up, so several can be on screen at once; a box that would overlap another stacks above it with a thin line down to its target. (Tried first: one hint at a time with a priority order, then with higher-priority hints cutting in; rejected because a nearby hint kept hiding another, e.g. the orb hint hiding the fuel tank's.) **Hints fade out of the way while you're close enough to do what they say** (owner's call: they were covering what you were doing): orb in tether reach, fuel tank in reach, orb right over the bowl, or low over the pad a landing hint points at. Move off and they fade back; being close doesn't use them up. Each stays until you do what it says (or a few seconds pass), fades, and never comes back on that visit to the level (dying doesn't repeat them). The hints:

- **Lift-off** (over the heli on the pad): HOLD ↑ TO LIFT OFF / ← → TO TILT, until you take off.
- **Fuel tank** (near one, not carrying, and only if your tank is under 95%, since Space does nothing over a tank when you're full): HOVER OVER A FUEL TANK / HOLD SPACE TO REFUEL.
- **Low fuel** (above the bottom-left fuel gauge, pointing down at it, below 25% while flying): LOW FUEL / FIND A FUEL TANK (GO EASY ON THE ROTOR if the level has no tanks left).
- **Orb** (near it): HOVER OVER THE ORB / HOLD SPACE TO TETHER IT.
- **Bowl** (near it while carrying): LOWER THE ORB IN / TAP SPACE TO LET GO.
- **Rescue pad** (near one with survivors waiting): RESCUE HOSTAGES (one line); stays until they're aboard (goes if you fly off, returns when you come back).
- **Fly back** (over the heli, right after the first delivery): ORB DELIVERED! / NOW FLY BACK TO YOUR PAD, with an arrow toward the pad, until the pad is in sight.
- **Enemies** (first balloon, turret or flying guard nearby, once enemies can shoot): Z TO FIRE · X TO TURN AROUND / HOLD SPACE FOR A SHIELD.
- **Home** (over your pad once it's in sight after delivery): LAND TO COMPLETE THE MISSION (one line; stays until you land). The only hint at the end: a separate "LAND ON YOUR PAD / TO DROP THEM OFF" for survivors aboard was removed (owner: one landing message is enough).

**Hints never cover a HUD box**: a hint that would overlap one slides sideways clear of it (sliding up or down left the box off-screen with a long line to its target; the owner disliked that line). Hints are tied to kinds of things, not positions, so they work on any level placed first. Keyboard labels only for now (gamepad players see keyboard keys).

## Front page, controls, deployed build

- **Front page** (added 2026-11-26 for the first public preview): THRUSTLIFTER logo over the picked level idling in the background, **PLAY**, a **starting-level picker** (◀ LEVEL n · NAME ▶) and **CONTROLS**. Mouse, keyboard (arrows / Enter, Esc back) and gamepad (d-pad or stick, A choose, B back, Start = play) all work.
- **Controls screen**: every action with two keyboard slots and one gamepad slot; click a slot, press the key or button. A key/button can only do one thing: binding it moves it off the other action (with a note saying so). Saved in the browser; RESET TO DEFAULTS. The left stick always tilts (and dives when pushed down); triggers are analog thrust/dive.
- **Default gamepad layout** (changed with remapping): RT thrust, LT dive, left stick / d-pad tilt, **A tether/refuel/shield**, X fire, B turn around, **Start pause, Select restart**. (Before: A thrust, LB tether, Start restart, Back pause.)
- **Tutorial hints name the player's own controls**, as gamepad buttons if they last used a pad.
- **Pause** lists the current controls, and **Q** (gamepad Y) quits to the front page.
- **Deployed build has no developer tools**: the level editor (E), tuning panel (T) and number-key level jumps exist only on the dev server (`import.meta.env.DEV`). Players choose a starting level on the front page instead. Hosting: the owner's Netlify (`netlify.toml`: `npm run build`, publish `dist`).

## Level editor

Press **E** in game to edit the current level (E again exits). Left toolbar: Select, Rock, **Cut rock (X)**, Launch pad, Rescue pad, Orb, Bowl, Turret, Balloon, Fuel tank, Survivor, Gate, Checkpoint (keys 1–9, 0, -, =; each Checkpoint click adds another one). Ground objects snap onto the surface; turrets mount on the nearest floor/wall/ceiling facing outward. Drag to move, **Cut rock (X)**: click a rock edge, then the edge across the rock, and it carves a passage straight through (default 160 wide, adjustable in the panel while the tool is active); the rock splits into two shapes with the gap between them, previewed as a green band (red if the line would cross open air). Needed because a level is often one big rock outline, and dragging points can never open a hole through it. **Join rock (J)**, the opposite (asked for when LAYUP had a crack from the ceiling down into its cave that couldn't be closed by dragging points: closing it would leave the cave as a hole inside one rock shape, which an outline can't describe): click a rock edge, then the edge across the gap, and it fills the gap with a new rock shape that hugs each wall for the **Fill width** (default 200, up to 2000; panel) centred on the clicks; green preview, red dashed line if the clicks don't cross open air. Works within one rock or between two. To make that look and play as one solid piece, **overlapping rocks act as one everywhere**: edges buried inside other rock (or where two rocks meet flush) aren't drawn, collided with, or shown on the minimap. Limitation: Cut works on one rock shape at a time, so cutting through a joined spot leaves the filler in place (cut the filler too, or delete its points). **Shift+click a rock edge to add a point** there and keep the button down to drag it into place (works with any tool except Rock/Gate; holding Shift shows a green + where it would go; Shift+click right on an existing point grabs that point instead); double-click an edge also adds one, Del to delete (a rescue pad's hostages are deleted with it, and move with it when you drag it; hostages left with no rescue pad nearby get a warning in the checks), Ctrl+Z/Y undo/redo, G grid snap, F frame all, C **tight spots** overlay (red = too narrow for the heli, amber = too low to carry the orb). Right panel: level settings (name, width, target time, launch-pad respawn fuel, cave backdrop), selected-object properties (pad guards, balloon drift, tank fuel, gate behaviour, each checkpoint's respawn fuel, **bowl size**) and live **checks** (missing pad/fuel minimums, checkpoint too close to the ground for the orb to hang below it, tank too close to the orb (only if some hover spot could reach both with Space; height counts, so a tank far above or below the orb is fine), buried/floating objects; errors block test/save). **Enter** test-flies the draft (E returns to the editor). **Save to project** (Ctrl+S) writes `src/levels/<name>.json` and adds new levels to the end of the play order (dev server only; a new level never overwrites an existing file); **Save as new copy** forks the current level into a new file; **Revert to saved** throws away unsaved edits (undoable). **Clear level** (asks first, undoable) gives a **blank canvas**: all rock and objects go, keeping the level's name, file and settings; only the orb and bowl stay where they were (every level needs exactly one of each) until you click them onto new ground with their tools. (First version reset to the New-level layout instead; the owner pointed out that's just New level, and wanted a truly blank canvas.) The editor draws a level with no launch pad yet, so the blank canvas shows as you build it; saving still needs a launch pad. **Bowl size** (owner's idea: a mechanic where you lob the orb into the bowl from a distance, which is only fair with a bigger target): 1–5× in quarter steps, default 1. The whole bowl scales evenly (cup width and depth, rim height, outside width), so a big bowl is also taller; the landing rule is unchanged (it still has to come to rest in the cup). A check warns if a big bowl pokes into rock at one end. Copy/Paste JSON also work. The editing session survives page reloads. **Not losing work** (after an ASTEROIDS level was lost: its save was blocked by an error, the brief "Can't save yet" message faded unnoticed, and exiting threw the draft away): while any error blocks saving, a red **CAN'T SAVE until fixed** box under the level title lists the errors, and stays until they're fixed; exiting the editor (E or the button) with unsaved changes asks first, and says why it can't be saved if that's the case.

**Organising levels:** the top of the right panel always says what you're editing (`LEVEL 2 of 3 · NAME`, `ON THE SHELF · NAME` or `NEW LEVEL (not saved)`, plus "● unsaved"). At the bottom, **Levels in the game** lists the play order: click a name to edit it, ▲▼ to reorder, ⤓ to take it out of the game. Levels taken out go on the **Shelf**: the file is kept untouched and still editable, just not played; ⤒ 1 puts it back as level 1, "+ end" as the last level. So replacing a level = save your new one, move it into place, shelve the old one. **Deleting:** a 🗑 button on shelved levels only (take a level out of the game first), with a confirm; it removes the file for good (git keeps anything that was committed). **File names follow level names**: saving a level whose name changed renames its file to match (same place in the play order); the editor shows names, never file names, but this keeps the folder tidy. Exiting the editor plays the level you were editing if it's in the game.

## Camera

Frames the ground under you (highest of 5 probes across a strip, eased, so gaps and cliff edges don't make it lurch) at "ground position on screen". In enclosed spaces it centres between floor and ceiling. Climbing shifts the view up to show what's above. The heli stays between 22% and 60% from the top, clear of the centre messages. "View height" is zoom.

## Open questions

- Should the rod collide with terrain (Thrust's didn't)?

- What a "harder level" means beyond tighter terrain: gravity per level, wind, heavier orbs, longer or shorter rods?
