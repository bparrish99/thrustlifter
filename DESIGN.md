# THRUSTLIFTER — Design

A tribute to the *feel* of the BBC Micro's Thrust, flown in a helicopter. Fly down,
pick up a heavy orb on a rigid rod, and lug it through increasingly tight terrain
to an orb bowl. The orb swings like a pendulum under the same gravity you fight,
and its momentum pushes and pulls the craft.

**Core principle: flying with the orb is the hardest part of the game. Combat never is,
no matter how hard a level gets.** Every system gets judged against that.

## Decided

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Platform | Browser, TypeScript, Vite. Canvas2D faux-vector glow for now (renderer isolated so a WebGL bloom pass can replace it). |
| Tilt | Limited to **±50°** (started at ±30°, opened up in playtesting). Input moves a *target* angle; actual tilt **eases** toward it. **No auto-level** (precise held angles matter in tight spaces). May revisit after playtesting. |
| Rotor | Thrust along the rotor normal. **Instant**: press = thrust, release = none (spool lag was tried and rejected because it made delicate moves too hard). Gamepad trigger gives analog throttle. Blades still *look* like they spin up. |
| Facing | Separate from tilt; flip at any time. Realistic nose geometry: tilt right + face right = nose down, missile fires slightly down. Flip to face left = nose up, fires up-left. |
| Dive | ↓ / S adds straight-down acceleration (world space, not along the rotor). Deliberately arcade, not realistic. Tunable. Shown by streaks blasting up out of the rotor (the downward version for normal thrust was rejected). |
| Missile (fire) | Fires straight out of the nose. Aim with tilt plus facing. |
| Bombs | **Removed for now** (one fire button is enough; Z fires, X flips). Possible later as upgrades, e.g. triple shot or firing perpendicular. |
| Tether | **Rigid rod** (rope makes it too easy). The rod is a true two-way constraint: the orb drags and shoves the heli. |
| Pickup | Hold tether (Space) within rod length of the orb, then pull away. After a release you must let go of Space before you can tether again. The rod locks at full length (Thrust-style; no "tractor beam" as a separate system). |
| Delivery | A small **orb bowl** (cup radius 28 vs orb radius 16). **Space while carrying releases the orb**, which keeps its momentum, so drops and "layups" work. **The bowl is the only thing the orb can touch safely**: it bounces around in the cup and is delivered once it **comes to rest in the bottom**. Within the bowl it's sturdy, shattering only on a head-on impact above **max impact** (default 240). Lowering it onto the bowl on the rod also works (a gentle touch auto-releases). |
| Orb destruction | The orb **explodes if it touches anything except the bowl** (ground, turrets), carried or dropped, **or if it's shot** (by anything, including your own weapons). **The pilot does not die.** The orb respawns on its pedestal after ~2s. |
| Mission complete | Choplifter-style: after the orb is in the bowl, **fly back and land on your start pad**. A HUD hint points the way home. |
| Collision | Heli touching anything = death (terrain, turrets, a loose orb). The exception is skids landing gently and level on a pad. The rod passes through terrain for now. The rod crossing the rotor = "rotor strike" death. |
| Shield | **Space does double duty:** hold = shield + tether beam + fuel-tank suction (Thrust-style) when not carrying; while carrying, no shield, tap = release. The shield absorbs enemy shots (not terrain). **Each Space press does exactly one thing, decided the moment it goes down, and never switches until released:** orb in reach → tether; else fuel tank in reach → refuel; else → shield; carrying → release. (E.g. a shield press stays a shield even over a tank; a tether press does nothing once the orb drifts out of reach.) |
| Fuel | Thrust (incl. dive) and the shield burn fuel; out of fuel = no thrust/shield. **Refuel Thrust-style from fuel tanks**: hover over one and hold Space to suck it up (limited amount; empties and vanishes; can be shot). **No refuel on death**, except topping up to the respawn point's minimum (see No soft-locks). **Each survivor who climbs aboard adds a little fuel**; pads never refuel. Low-fuel warning beeps. Tunable ("Fuel & shield" folder). |
| Survivors | Choplifter-style **optional bonus**: little figures waiting near green **rescue pads** (pads exist only for rescues; each survivor picked up brings a small fuel boost) wave at you, run aboard when you land nearby, and count as rescued when you land on your start pad. Crash with them aboard and they're lost. Shown as RESCUED x/y and on the mission-complete line. |
| Safe start | **Until you lift off from the launch pad** (at level start and after every pad respawn), enemies hold fire, guards stay parked, and any shot already in flight fizzles against you. |
| No soft-locks | Every respawn point has a **minimum fuel** in level data (`startFuelMin` for the launch pad, `checkpoints[].fuel` after delivery), sized so the next stretch is always possible. **Landing with an empty tank strands you**: after ~2.5s it ends the attempt (counts as a death, no explosion, "STRANDED"), anyone aboard climbs back out, and you respawn at your checkpoint with its minimum fuel. New levels must set both minimums (tested). |
| Checkpoints | **Internal, never announced.** Die before delivering: back to the launch pad. Die after delivering: respawn hovering at the level's `delivery` checkpoint (default: above the bowl), **held in place until you touch a control** (turrets hold fire meanwhile). Closes the "crash, hop, land" exploit. |
| Out of scope | No reactor, no inverse gravity. |
| Gravity | Gentle, Thrust-like. Playtest-tuned defaults: gravity 230, thrust 645, max horizontal speed 335, dive 170, max tilt 50°. The first defaults (320/850) were far too violent. With the orb, full thrust holds altitude up to ~43° of tilt; past that (up to the 50° max) you sink. |
| Turrets | Armoured gun emplacements (bolted base plate, faceted metal dome, twin barrels with a muzzle block that glow when charging, a sensor slit that blinks fast while tracking). Big enough to hit with dumb-fire missiles (generous hit area). They track you, **telegraph each shot with a visible charge-up glow**, then fire a slow, dodgeable shot. They need line of sight, range and a firing arc around the way they face, so they can sit on floors, ceilings or walls. A hit kills you or destroys the orb. Range, interval, charge time, shot speed, aim lead and arc are tunable ("Enemies" folder). Combat stays secondary to flying. **Placement rule: only put turrets where the player can dodge and shoot back.** You can't fire upward (missiles stay within ±45° of horizontal), and there are no shields yet, so no turrets above you in vertical shafts or in spots you're forced through. |
| Guard helicopters | Red enemy helis **parked on rescue pads, holding the survivors hostage**. Each pad has a set number of guards (`guards`, default 2 in level data); when you come within range with line of sight, one spins up, lifts off and hunts you: keeps a standoff distance, telegraphs each shot with a nose glow, and stays leashed near its pad. One missile downs one; the next appears after a delay (but never while you're within the spawn clearance of its pad, so it can't pop up right next to you); when they're all gone the pad is clear. **Once a pad's survivors have been picked up, no more guards come** (one already flying keeps fighting). They die hitting terrain; colliding with one kills you (the orb destroys them too, and is lost). `guardsAfter: delivery` keeps a pad's guards asleep until the orb is delivered (keeps the carry enemy-free). Tunable in "Enemies". |
| Balloons | Angular, armoured enemy gasbags (faceted red body, fins, top spike, a glowing red eye slit that glances at you and flares when charging, rigid strut to a hex gun pod; classic hot-air look rejected) drift in a slow loop around their spot (`patrol`), bobbing, with a swivelling gun under the basket that tracks you, telegraphs with a glow, and fires when it sees you in range. One missile pops one. Touching one kills you; the orb pops one but is lost. Tunable in "Enemies". Level 2 keeps them on the surface so the carry stays enemy-free (tested). |
| Gates | Energy barriers in level data: `closes: pickup` seals the way in when you grab the orb; `opens: delivery` opens a way out when it's delivered. They reset if you die before delivering. This is how levels force a separate return path. |
| Levels | Played in order (`src/levels/index.ts`). N goes to the next level after completing one; 1-9 jump to any level. Level 1 Proving Ground (surface); Level 2 Deep Caverns: the orb sits right at the bottom of the first shaft, then a long winding carry (passage, drop shaft, lower tunnel, climb, big chamber) to the bowl with **no enemies on the carry**; turrets guard only the way in and the way home (sealing gate on pickup, exit gate up a separate shaft on delivery). **Level-design rule: the orb comes early, and the orb-to-bowl stretch is the main challenge.** Cave levels set `backdropY` so open cave reads dark against solid rock. |
| Sound | Procedural (Web Audio, no samples). Rotor chop tempo follows the rotor: a brisk, soft but clearly audible idle chop (~8/s) building to a harder chop (~15/s) at full throttle, over a steady drone with a light low rumble and faint turbine whine. Treble kept soft (it read as a machine gun when bright). The idle chop must sit in an audible band, not just sub-bass. M mutes; volume in the tuning panel. Event effects are synthesized too, and must have **weight, depth and sustain** (thin, short "bubble-wrap pops" were rejected). They're layered: explosions = crack + darkening fiery body + long brown-noise rumble + sub-bass drop; metal rings for clunks and debris; a rising "sucking" hum (boooeeee...) swoops up while the tether beam pulls, ending in an "...up!" chirp at lock-on (phaser tones were tried and rejected). All effects go through a hall reverb and a compressor and ring out for 1–3.5s. Distant events play quieter. |
| HUD | Minimal, big and flashy, in neon corner brackets: **score** (pops when it changes) + **lives** (heli icons) top-left; **TIME BONUS countdown**, **RESCUED x/y** and an **ORB lamp** (pulses when delivered) top-right; a **LED-bar fuel gauge** bottom-centre: realistic LED lenses (hot core, specular glint, halo; dark unlit lenses still catch light), red to amber to yellow to green, blinks when low, a light sweeps along it while refuelling, no label. Big centre messages for events; each level opens with its name. (A round analog dial was tried and rejected.) |
| Progression | After the mission-complete tally and a ~3s pause on the results, the **next level starts automatically** (lives and score carry over). After the last level it stays on the results (R to play again). |
| Lives | Start with **3**; each death (crash or stranded) costs one; at zero → **GAME OVER** (R retries the level with 3 lives). Lives and score carry to the next level. Extra life every 10,000 points. |
| Score | Turret **100**, balloon **200**, guard heli **300** (player kills: missiles or the orb), **500** per survivor rescued (dropped at your pad), and **10 per tenth of a second of TIME BONUS left** at mission complete. Bonus time starts at the level's `targetTime` (L1 90s, L2 150s for now), **counts down only once you first lift off**, and just stops at zero (no penalty). The HUD timer shows only this countdown, labelled TIME BONUS. Plus **25 per whole tick of fuel left** on the 24-LED gauge. **No on-screen "+N" popups**; instead, on mission complete a **bonus tally** plays in the centre of the screen: the TIME BONUS clock drains into the score (rising blips), then the fuel gauge drains tick by tick (a climbing "ka-ching" per tick), then a final chord and summary. **Extra life every 10,000 points** (1UP! flash + arpeggio). |
| Sky | Dark gradient with twinkling stars and a faint, generated **nebula** (wispy magenta/violet/teal/amber clouds with dark dust lanes, slow parallax) behind them. The cartoon ringed planet was removed. Cave levels' dark backdrop covers it underground. |
| Ground | Solid rock has **body**: a generated tileable stone texture (mottled, veined, relief-shaded, grain) darkening with depth, with the neon surface edge and a lit band just inside it. (Neon line decorations inside the rock were tried and rejected.) |
| Pads | Your launch/return pad is **steady green**. Rescue pads are enemy pads: **blinking red**, turning **blinking yellow** once you're within the guard spawn clearance (no new guard will appear). |
| Orb pedestal | The orb starts on a glowing magenta-accented plinth generated under its start position (levels only place the orb). Solid like terrain; the glow dims once the orb is lifted. |
| Labels | **Show, don't tell.** The only centre messages are the level name at the start, MISSION COMPLETE (with the bonus tally) and GAME OVER; everything else (crashes, orb secured/destroyed/delivered, rescues, stranded) is shown through visuals and sound. **No text on level elements** (no ORB BOWL / RESCUE / FUEL words); shapes and colours carry the meaning. Text lives only in the HUD and centre messages. |
| Look | Colourful faux-vector 80s arcade with modern glitz: additive glow lines, bloom, particles, scanlines, vignette. |

## Core loop

Launch from your pad → fight through the level → grab the orb → deliver it to the bowl → make your way back and land on your pad.

Level-design hook: the return trip doesn't have to retrace the outbound route. Levels can force a **separate path from the bowl back to the pad** (one-way drops, gates that open on delivery, a route that only works without the orb's extra length and weight).

## Milestones

- **M0, lug test** (current): one surface level, heli, orb, rod, orb bowl, inert turrets, missiles/bombs, live tuning panel. *Goal: is carrying the orb fun?*
- **M1, combat basics**: turrets that shoot ✅, shields + fuel ✅, survivors ✅, checkpoints ✅, guard helicopters ✅ (proof of concept).
- **M2, caverns**: underground levels ✅ (Deep Caverns), level sequencing ✅, gates ✅, a level editor.
- **M3, glitz pass**: WebGL bloom, audio, screen effects, menus.

## Camera

Frames the ground under you (highest of 5 probes across a strip, eased, so gaps and cliff edges don't make it lurch) at "ground position on screen". In enclosed spaces it centres between floor and ceiling. Climbing shifts the view up to show what's above. The heli stays between 22% and 60% from the top, clear of the centre messages. "View height" is zoom.

## Open questions
